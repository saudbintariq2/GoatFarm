Green fodder cost

cost the land, not the kilos
You cannot weigh fresh-cut fodder — so don't try. Put in what the fodder land costs you for a year, and the app spreads it over the herd to give a real cost per goat per day. Nothing is charged twice: these amounts are already in your Running costs — here they are only allocated to feed so your cost per goat is complete.

Feed plan

set once — what one goat eats per day
One row per group — set what one goat in that group eats per day, and the cost works itself out. Press Recipe to change what goes into that group's mix.
Mix kg — concentrate you buy; priced from that group's recipe.
Green kg — an estimate, not a price. It does two jobs: tells you how much to cut each day, and splits the fodder-land cost fairly (a doe on 2 kg carries four times what a kid on 0.5 kg does).
Dry kg — toori/bhoosa you buy; priced per kg.
Fodder Rs/day — that group's share of the land cost, worked out for you.
Medicine and vaccines are not planned here — you buy them as needed, so they are recorded as real purchases on the Vaccines tab or as a Running cost. A month with none costs nothing.

Adjusting rations: type straight into any box — the cost, buying list and stock all follow immediately. A rough starting point for Beetal-type goats, to change against your own vet's advice and what the animals actually clear up:
Kids — very little concentrate, mostly milk and soft green · Milking — the heaviest ration; feed to yield · Pregnant — moderate, then increase in the last 6 weeks (more again for twins) · Dry — the leanest; mostly fodder to hold condition · Bucks — moderate, lift before the breeding season · For sale — push concentrate to finish them.
Feed refused in the trough means the ration is too high; hungry animals and falling milk mean it is too low.

Capital vs running cost: things you own (goats you bought, machinery, land, sheds) are capital — added once. Things you use up each month (feed, salaries, cultivation) are running costs. Profit = cash received − running costs. Capital is your investment, kept separate.
